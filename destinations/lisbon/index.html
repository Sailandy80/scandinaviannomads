Portugal · Europe

Lisbon

Tiled facades, river light and boutique stays for a fraction of Paris prices.

Why go

Lisbon delivers what most travellers pay a premium for elsewhere: great food, design-led boutique hotels, rooftop views and a mild climate, all at prices that are still reasonable by Western European standards. It is also a short flight from Scandinavia, which makes it ideal for a long weekend.

Best time to visit

  • April to June: warm, green and lively before peak summer prices.
  • Mid-September to October: still beach weather nearby, the sea at its warmest, and hotel rates dropping.
  • July and August: hot and busy; book ahead or stay on the Cascais line instead.
  • Winter: mild and quiet, with the best hotel deals of the year.

Where to stay

Look for a boutique hotel or design guesthouse in Alfama, Chiado, Príncipe Real or Baixa. Small independent hotels here regularly offer a level of style you would pay double for in London or Paris. Ask for a room with a river view and check whether breakfast is included; it often tips the value.

What to eat

Pastéis de nata (warm, with cinnamon), grilled sardines in summer, bacalhau, bifana sandwiches, and fresh seafood. Many of the city's better restaurants run excellent-value set lunches on weekdays.

How to save

  • Eat the prato do dia. The dish of the day at local tascas is one of Europe's best-value meals.
  • Use public transport. Trams, metro and trains are cheap; a rechargeable transport card covers the city and the line to Cascais and Sintra.
  • Go to viewpoints, not rooftop bars. Miradouros like Senhora do Monte and Santa Luzia have the same views for free.
  • Visit Sintra early on a weekday. You beat the crowds and the queues.

Sample 3-day itinerary

Day 1 – Old Lisbon. Alfama on foot, the castle, sunset at a miradouro, dinner at a family-run tasca.

Day 2 – Belém and LX Factory. Jerónimos Monastery, the original pastéis de Belém, the riverside to MAAT, evening at LX Factory.

Day 3 – Sintra or Cascais. Palaces and gardens in Sintra, or the beach and seafood in Cascais. Back for a final dinner in Príncipe Real.

Budget luxury score: 8.6

Boutique hotels, great food and sunshine for less than you would expect. One of Europe's easiest budget-luxury wins.
