Japan · Asia

Kyoto

Temples, gardens and ryokan calm, without the ryokan price every night.

Why go

Kyoto is the Japan most people dream of: wooden townhouses, moss gardens, Zen temples and an exceptional food culture. A weak yen in recent years has also made high-quality Japanese hospitality more accessible to European travellers than it has been for a long time.

Best time to visit

  • Late March to early April: cherry blossom. Beautiful, crowded and expensive; book months ahead.
  • November: autumn leaves, equally popular.
  • January to February and early June: fewer visitors and better hotel rates.
  • July and August: hot and humid, with summer festivals.

Where to stay

Mix and match. Stay most nights in a modern design hotel or business hotel near Kyoto Station or Shijō, then splurge on one night in a traditional ryokan with dinner and breakfast included. You get the full experience at a fraction of the cost of a ryokan-only trip.

What to eat

Kaiseki (try it at lunch), tofu and yuba dishes, matcha everything, Nishiki Market snacks, and ramen. Convenience-store breakfasts in Japan are genuinely good and very cheap.

How to save

  • Have kaiseki at lunch. Many restaurants serve a shorter lunch version for much less.
  • Go early. Fushimi Inari and Arashiyama at sunrise are calmer and free or cheap.
  • Use buses and walk. Kyoto is compact; an IC card works on buses and trains.
  • Check rail pass maths. The Japan Rail Pass is now less of an automatic win; compare with individual tickets for your route.

Sample 3-day itinerary

Day 1 – Eastern Kyoto. Kiyomizu-dera early, Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka lanes, Gion at dusk.

Day 2 – Arashiyama. Bamboo grove at sunrise, Tenryū-ji garden, Ōkōchi Sansō villa, river views. Night in a ryokan.

Day 3 – Fushimi Inari and markets. The torii gates early, Nishiki Market lunch, Philosopher's Path in the afternoon.

Budget luxury score: 8.4

One ryokan night, lunch-time kaiseki and early starts give you a refined Kyoto for a sensible budget.
