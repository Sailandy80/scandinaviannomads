Black Friday has become one of the busiest booking days of the year for hotels, airlines and tour operators. Some of the offers are excellent. Many are ordinary prices with a countdown clock. Here is how to tell the difference before you pay.

## 1. Know the normal price first

A discount only means something if you know what it is a discount from. Before you book, check the same room or route for the same dates on the hotel's or airline's own website, and on one or two comparison sites. If you have been watching a trip for a while, set a price alert now so you have a baseline next time.

## 2. Read the travel dates, not just the headline

The biggest savings are often limited to quiet periods: weekdays, low season or a narrow window months ahead. That can still be a great deal, as long as those dates actually suit you. A cheap stay you cannot use is not a bargain.

## 3. Check what you can change or cancel

Sale rates are frequently non-refundable. Compare the sale price with the flexible rate on the same booking page. If the gap is small, flexibility is usually worth paying for. If it is large and your plans are firm, the sale rate can make sense.

## 4. Look for value, not just a lower number

The best budget-luxury deals often add something rather than cut the price: breakfast included, a room upgrade, a resort or spa credit, a free night on a longer stay, or extra loyalty points. Work out what those extras would cost you separately.

## 5. Book direct when the price is the same

If a hotel matches the price you found elsewhere, booking directly often makes it easier to ask for an early check-in or a better room, and to sort out any problems later. It also helps your loyalty status if you are a member.

## Do not let the countdown decide for you

Similar offers usually return around Cyber Monday, in January sales and in airline flash sales during the year. If a deal does not pass the five checks above, let it go.

**The budget-luxury takeaway:** decide where and when you want to travel first, then use Black Friday to pay less for that trip, not to buy a trip you did not plan.
