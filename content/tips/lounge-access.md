A lounge turns a stressful airport into a calm start to your trip: a comfortable seat, food, drinks and often a shower. You do not need a business-class ticket to get in.

## Ways in

- **Airline status.** Higher tiers in programmes such as SAS EuroBonus give lounge access, often even on economy tickets, depending on the airline and alliance rules.
- **Credit cards.** Some premium cards include lounge memberships or a number of free visits per year. Check whether the annual fee makes sense for how often you fly.
- **Lounge memberships.** Programmes such as Priority Pass give access to independent lounges worldwide for an annual fee or per-visit charge.
- **Pay at the door or prebook.** Many independent lounges sell single visits. Prebooking online is often cheaper.
- **Paid upgrades.** Some airlines sell lounge access as an add-on when you book.

## Is it worth it?

Work out the real value: what would you otherwise spend on food and drinks at the airport, and how much is calm and a quiet place to work worth on a long layover? For a long connection or an early departure, a single paid visit is often good value. For a short, direct flight, it rarely is.

## Tips

- Check the lounge's hours and location; some are in a different terminal.
- Lounges can be full at peak times, and some programmes do not guarantee entry.
- Look for the "premium" independent lounges; quality varies a lot.

**The budget-luxury takeaway:** a well-chosen lounge visit is one of the cheapest ways to make a trip feel first class.
