Business class on points: a beginner's guide for Scandinavians

How to start collecting points in Norway, Sweden and Denmark and turn them into a lie-flat seat.

Published 23 November 2026

Flying business class long-haul is the single biggest "luxury upgrade" most travellers dream of, and points are the most realistic way to do it without paying full fare. You do not need to be a frequent flyer. You need a plan.

1. Pick one main programme

Scattering points across five programmes gets you nowhere. For most Scandinavians the natural starting point is SAS EuroBonus, since SAS is the region's main carrier and EuroBonus points can be earned through flights, partners and everyday spending. If you mostly fly with another alliance, choose that airline's programme instead. The rule is simple: concentrate.

2. Earn on everyday spending

The fastest points rarely come from flying. Look at:

  • Co-branded credit cards that earn airline points on purchases. Pay the full balance every month, or the interest wipes out any value.
  • Shopping, hotel and car-rental partners of your programme.
  • Sign-up bonuses when you genuinely need a new card or service.

3. Learn what an award seat really costs

Award seats are priced in points plus taxes and fees. On some routes the cash part is small; on others (especially from some European airports) it is significant. Always compare the total with a paid fare before booking.

4. Be flexible on dates and routes

Award availability is limited. You will find far more seats if you:

  • search a range of dates rather than one day,
  • consider a nearby departure airport,
  • book early (many programmes open their calendars about a year ahead) or very late, when unsold seats are sometimes released.

5. Use partners

Alliance partners often release award seats you can book with your own points. Learn which partners your programme uses and search their availability too.

6. Start small

Your first redemption does not have to be a 12-hour flight. A short-haul business or a one-way long-haul leg is a great way to learn the system.

The budget-luxury takeaway: pick one programme, earn on spending you would do anyway, stay flexible, and always compare points-plus-fees with the cash price.
